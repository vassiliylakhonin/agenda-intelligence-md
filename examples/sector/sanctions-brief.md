# Example: Sanctions Sector Lens

**Bottom line:** Treat this as an enforcement marker if it includes official action, named parties, or regulator guidance. Treat it as a weak signal if it is only media reporting or political rhetoric.

**Sanctions signal classification:** Signal / possible enforcement marker.

**What changed:** The development may shift compliance burden from general monitoring to specific counterparty, routing, or documentation checks.

**Authority and instrument:** Identify whether the source is OFAC, EU, UK OFSI, UN, export-control authority, customs agency, or another regulator. Legal weight depends on the authority and instrument.

**Affected parties or flows:** Banks, exporters, logistics firms, insurers, brokers, beneficial owners, vessels, dual-use goods, payment chains, or trade-finance channels.

**Exposure mechanism:** Could arise through ownership/control, prohibited transaction, routing, export-control classification, insurance, facilitation, or reputational exposure.

**Compliance impact:** Higher due diligence burden, possible account closures, contract review, payment delays, route redesign, licensing review, or counterparty screening escalation.

**Main uncertainty:** Whether this is a documented enforcement pathway or a warning signal that does not yet change legal obligations.

**Scenarios:**
- **Low impact:** guidance remains broad; firms update monitoring only.
- **Operational impact:** banks, insurers, or logistics providers change behavior.
- **Enforcement impact:** named parties or transactions trigger penalties, designations, seizures, or contract exits.

**Watch next:** official legal text, regulator FAQs, designations, penalties, customs detentions, named intermediaries, correspondent banking behavior, insurance withdrawal, and route or payment redesigns.
