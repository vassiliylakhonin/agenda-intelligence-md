# Before / After: Red Sea shipping disruption

## Scenario

A user asks:

> There are new reports of attacks affecting Red Sea shipping. What does this mean for companies?

## Before: generic agent output

Recent Red Sea attacks are concerning because the Red Sea is a major global shipping route. Disruptions could affect supply chains, increase shipping costs, and create delays for companies. Businesses should monitor the situation and consider alternative routes where necessary.

The situation also has geopolitical implications because several regional actors are involved. Continued instability could affect trade, energy markets, and regional security. Companies should stay informed and prepare contingency plans.

## What is wrong with the before version

- It says “monitor” without naming indicators.
- It treats all companies as equally exposed.
- It does not distinguish a one-off incident from a sustained chokepoint risk.
- It ignores insurance, rerouting, naval posture, and compliance exposure.
- It gives no escalation logic.

## After: with Agenda-Intelligence.md

**Bottom line:** This is an escalation marker if attacks are repeated, claimed credibly, or followed by shipping/insurance behavior. A single incident is not enough; the company impact depends on whether the route becomes predictably unsafe or legally/commercially costly.

**Signal classification:** Escalation marker / possible trigger event.

**What changed:** The risk shifts from geopolitical background noise to operational exposure when shipping lines, insurers, ports, or governments change behavior.

**Why it matters:** The business impact comes through freight cost, route timing, insurance premiums, force majeure risk, inventory planning, and contractual delivery obligations. For some firms, compliance and sanctions screening may also matter if counterparties or armed networks are implicated.

**Who is affected:**
- importers/exporters using Asia-Europe routes;
- shipping and logistics firms;
- insurers and trade-finance providers;
- energy and commodity traders;
- companies with tight delivery windows or low inventory buffers.

**Energy or chokepoint implications:** Watch Bab el-Mandeb, Red Sea route advisories, Suez Canal traffic, rerouting around the Cape of Good Hope, and insurance premium changes. The chokepoint matters only if behavior changes at scale.

**Main uncertainty:** Whether this remains episodic harassment or becomes a sustained route-risk regime.

**Scenarios:**
- **Contained:** incidents continue but major carriers keep routing with higher precautions.
- **Commercial disruption:** carriers reroute, insurance reprices, delivery times lengthen.
- **Escalation:** military response or broader regional retaliation increases uncertainty beyond shipping.

**Watch next:** carrier route announcements, war-risk insurance rates, naval advisories, Suez traffic data, port delays, credible claims of responsibility, and government force-protection changes.

## Why the after is better

| Criterion | Before | After |
|---|---|---|
| Signal classification | Missing | Present |
| What changed | Vague | Specific delta identified |
| Actor specificity | Generic | Segmented by exposed groups |
| Uncertainty | Hidden or generic | Main uncertainty named |
| Falsifiability | Missing | Watch indicators can confirm or weaken the view |
| Watch-next | “Monitor developments” | Concrete indicators listed |
