# UK-EU post-Brexit regulatory divergence

**Signal classification:** signal
**Evidence mode:** mixed

## Bottom line

UK diverges from EU on AI regulation and chemical safety standards, creating dual-compliance burden for firms operating in both markets.

## What changed

UK government published its own AI regulatory framework rejecting the EU's risk-based approach in favor of sector-specific regulation.

## Why it matters

Companies must maintain separate compliance programs for UK and EU markets; cost and complexity increase for cross-border operations.

## Main uncertainty

UK framework is principles-based and largely unlegislated; actual compliance requirements depend on sector regulators.

## Affected actors

- UK government
- European Commission
- cross-border tech firms
- financial services firms
- chemical manufacturers

## Scenarios

**Pragmatic alignment:** UK regulators adopt requirements functionally similar to EU despite different framing.
- Cross-referencing of EU standards in UK guidance
- mutual recognition agreements

**Deepening divergence:** UK deliberately differentiates to attract investment; compliance arbitrage opportunities emerge.
- Lower UK compliance costs
- UK-only product approvals

## Watch next

- UK sector regulator AI guidance publications
- Mutual recognition negotiations
- Industry compliance cost surveys
