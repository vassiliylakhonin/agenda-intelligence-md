# Critical minerals export controls

**Signal classification:** structural_shift
**Evidence mode:** live_source_backed

## Bottom line

China restricts export of gallium, germanium, and rare earth processing technology, creating structural supply risk for semiconductor and defense supply chains.

## What changed

China Ministry of Commerce announced export licensing requirements for gallium, germanium, antimony, and related processing technology.

## Why it matters

China controls 60-90% of global processing for these minerals; licensing creates discretionary supply disruption tool.

## Main uncertainty

Licensing may be selectively enforced; alternative supply development timelines are 3-7 years.

## Affected actors

- China MOFCOM
- semiconductor manufacturers
- defense contractors
- mining companies outside China
- Japan/EU/US industrial policy agencies

## Scenarios

**Selective restriction:** Licenses granted to allied countries, denied to others.
- Differentiated approval rates by destination country
- diplomatic linkage to other trade disputes

**Broad restriction:** Export volumes drop across all destinations.
- Global price spikes for gallium/germanium
- emergency stockpile releases

## Watch next

- Export license approval rates published by MOFCOM
- Stockpile purchases by Japan/EU/US
- Alternative processing facility investment announcements
