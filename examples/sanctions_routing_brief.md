# Real-World Brief Example: Sanctions Rerouting Through Central Asia (2025)

**Bottom line:** EU/US secondary sanctions are pushing Russian-linked trade into Central Asian corridors (Kazakhstan, Kyrgyzstan, Uzbekistan), creating a "grey‑zone" that requires intensified due‑diligence and monitoring of SMEs in the region.

**Signal classification:** structural shift

**What changed:**  
- New EU guidance (2025) explicitly lists common re‑export red‑flags for Central Asia.  
- US OFAC issued a "sanctions‑evasion" advisory targeting Turkic‑speaking transit hubs.  
- Kazakhstan introduced a "beneficial‑ownership transparency" register (Jan 2026) that *still* has gaps for offshore shells.

**Why it matters:**  
- Logistics and fintech companies moving goods through the region face **higher compliance costs** and reputational risk.  
- Banks in Almaty, Bishkek, and Tashkent must upgrade KYC/AML filters or risk losing SWIFT access.  
- Successful evasion routes become **precedents** for future sanctions designations.

**Who is affected:**  
- Logistics providers (Maersk, DPD, local rail operators).  
- Regional banks (Halyk Bank, KICB, IPak Yuli Bank).  
- SMEs acting as "middle‑men" for dual‑use goods.  
- EU/US exporters monitoring their supply‑chain footprints.

**Main uncertainty:**  
- Whether Russia will formalise a "parallel‑import" corridor with Central Asian states.  
- Speed of adoption for the new beneficial‑ownership register (technical + political hurdles).  
- Extent of US "secondary" enforcement – will it stretch to small‑trade IGUs?

**Scenarios:**  
1. **Enforcement tightens** – EU blacklists 3‑5 "high‑risk" Central Asian SMEs, forcing re‑routing again.  
2. **Regional compromise** – a trilateral (EU‑KZ‑UZ) monitoring cell is created, easing compliance burdens for vetted firms.  
3. **Gap exploitation** – opaque Kyrgyzstan free‑economic zones become the new primary evasion hub.

**Watch next:**  
- First EU “compliance watchlist” publication (expected Q3 2026).  
- OFAC advisory follow‑up designations (any Central Asian entity).  
- Kazakhstan’s beneficial‑ownership register first audit report.  
- Spike in “rail freight via Turkmenistan” spot rates (early indicator of another route shift).  
- Policy papers from the Eurasian Development Bank on “sanctions‑proof” logistics.
