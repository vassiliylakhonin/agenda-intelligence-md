## A. Pains the v1 contract already addresses

For each pain: verbatim quote, source, the contract element that absorbs it.

### A1. FFI sanctions risk under EO 14114 — the seven-factor significance test

Calibration note: the "unknowingly facilitate" framing in FPRI is the author's emphasis, not an OFAC quote. The actual OFAC mechanism is the seven-factor "significant transaction" test in FAQ 1151, where management awareness is **one** of seven factors, not dispositive:

> "(a) the size, number, and frequency of the transaction(s); (b) the nature of the transaction(s); (c) the level of awareness of management and whether the transactions are part of a pattern of conduct; (d) the nexus of the transaction(s) to persons sanctioned pursuant to E.O. 14024, or to persons operating in Russia's military-industrial base; (e) whether the transaction(s) involve deceptive practices; (f) the impact of the transaction(s) on U.S. national security objectives; and (g) such other relevant factors that OFAC deems relevant."

Source: OFAC FAQ 1151 (updated June 12, 2024).

Covered by:

- `evidence_gaps[]` surfaces what is not yet known — addresses factor (b) and (d) gaps.
- `human_review_required: true` forces a human gate before commercial action.
- `not_advice_notice` blocks claims-of-clearance.
- Evidence-mode discipline rule ([docs/evidence-modes.md](../evidence-modes.md)): every `basis: "fact"` provenance entry must carry a `source` or end the claim with `[verify]`. Sanctions designations, vessel SDN entries, and regulatory effective dates are explicitly fixture-tested as the categories most likely to be wrongly stated as bare facts.

Gap surfaced: the seven factors are not individually structured in the response. See **B10** below.

- Dated sources are required per request, not optional.

## B. Gaps where the contract is silent — candidates for the smallest honest change

Each gap names the pain, what is already in the schema, and the minimum non-breaking addition that would close it. All are subject to live-voice validation before becoming a PR.

### B1. Sanctions-list regime and version on the supplied extract

Pain (inferred from Wolfsberg + OFAC FFI guidance): an auditor asks "which OFAC SDN version was this screened against, and on which date?" The deal file needs to show it screened OFAC, OFSI, EU consolidated, KZ FMA, and so on.

Current contract: `sanctions_list_extract` is a source type. The dated-source object carries `date` and `url` but no `regime` or `list_version` field.

Smallest honest change: optional `regime` (enum: `OFAC_SDN`, `OFAC_SSI`, `OFSI`, `EU_consolidated`, `UN_consolidated`, `KZ_FMA`, `other`) and optional `list_version` (string) on dated sources of type `sanctions_list_extract`. Non-breaking. Lets the response surface "OFAC covered as of 2026-05-21, OFSI missing" in `evidence_gaps`.

### B2. Multi-regime coverage attestation per supplied extract

Pain: one supplied sanctions extract may cover multiple regimes at once (e.g. a vendor's blended list). Today the gap surface is binary supplied / missing for a single `sanctions_list_extract`, which is structurally lossy.

Current contract: single `sanctions_list_extract` source type.

Smallest honest change: optional `regimes_covered: string[]` per `sanctions_list_extract` (enum same as B1). Lets the worker compute "OFAC + EU covered, OFSI missing" without forcing the user to upload three separate files.

### B3. 50% rule / ownership-chain depth declaration

Pain: OFAC's 50% rule attaches sanctions to entities ≥50% owned, directly or indirectly, by an SDN. Walking the ownership chain is the most failure-prone EDD step for KZ counterparties with possible Russian indirect ownership. Today the contract knows whether a `beneficial_ownership_source` was supplied — not how deep the chain was walked.

Current contract: `beneficial_ownership_source` source type exists.

Smallest honest change: an optional `ownership_chain_indicators: string[]` on `counterparty_readiness` (same pattern as `vessel_due_diligence_indicators`), drawn from OFAC 50% rule guidance — e.g. "Direct ownership chain walked to ≥2 levels", "UBO identified", "Indirect ≥50% aggregation checked", "Last-checked date present". Evidence-gap checklist routed to human review. Not a 50% rule determination.

### B4. Affirmative-defense record for "unknowingly facilitate"

Pain: under EO 14114, the affirmative defense an FFI relies on is "we checked X against list Y at time Z and found nothing." The `document_ledger` already records `date_received` per source type, but it is framed as dossier-completeness, not as the affirmative-defense record.

Current contract: `document_ledger` exists.

Smallest honest change: documentation, not schema. Add a section in [docs/use-cases/kazakhstan-middle-corridor.md](../use-cases/kazakhstan-middle-corridor.md) (and the CIS secondary-sanctions use-case doc) that says explicitly: the `document_ledger` plus `screening_run_timestamp` per entry IS the affirmative-defense record. If that needs structuring beyond `date_received`, add an optional `screening_run_timestamp` on `document_ledger` entries. Non-breaking.

### B5. Source-list staleness threshold

Pain: a supplied source dated 90 days ago does not satisfy continuous-monitoring expectations. Today the worker can compute staleness from `dated_source.date` but there is no contract-side declared per-source-type threshold.

Current contract: dated-source `date` exists; no `staleness_threshold_days`.

Smallest honest change: not contract — worker-layer. The Deal Risk Gate worker already ships counterparty-readiness logic; the same layer can surface "sanctions_list_extract older than 30 days" as an `evidence_gaps` entry without any schema change. Out of scope for the contract layer; in scope for the worker.

### B6. Counterparty sector tagging (the named five)

Pain: OFAC FAQ 1148 / 1151 names five sectors as automatic high-risk:

> "any person operating in the following sectors of the Russian Federation economy: technology, defense and related materiel, construction, aerospace, or manufacturing"

A KZ-domiciled counterparty operating in one of these sectors is a direct EO 14024 exposure point that the FFI must surface.

Current contract: `counterparty` has `role`, `name`, `jurisdiction`, free-text `notes`. No structured sector field.

Smallest honest change: optional `specified_sectors[]` on counterparty, enum drawn verbatim from FAQ 1151 (`technology`, `defense_and_related_materiel`, `construction`, `aerospace`, `manufacturing`, `other`). Non-breaking. Lets the worker surface "counterparty in named sector → escalation" in `evidence_gaps` and `exposure_layers.foreign_sanctions_exposure_layer`.

### B7. Counterparty date of formation

Pain: OFAC FFI advisory red-flag example:

> "EXAMPLE OF HIGHER RISK CUSTOMER: A microelectronics exporter formed in March 2022 located in a high-risk jurisdiction that has received repeated international wire transfers from possible shell companies in offshore financial centers."

Date-of-formation post-Feb-2022 in a transshipment jurisdiction is a named red flag. The current `counterparty` object cannot carry this fact structurally.

Current contract: no date-of-formation field on counterparty.

Smallest honest change: optional `date_of_formation` (ISO date string) on counterparty. Non-breaking. Lets the worker surface "counterparty formed after 2022-02-24 in a named-hub jurisdiction" as a structured indicator rather than free-text notes.

### B8. Counterparty attestation as a first-class evidence artifact

Pain: OFAC FFI advisory explicitly names attestations as an FFI mitigation control:

> "When appropriate, obtaining attestations from high-risk customers that they do not operate in the specified sectors, engage in any sales or transfers of the specified items to Russia, or otherwise conduct any transactions involving Russia's military-industrial base (including involving any person blocked pursuant to E.O. 14024)."

The contract's `source_type` enum carries `user_provided_note` (too generic) and `contract_or_invoice_extract` (wrong category). A counterparty attestation is a distinct EDD artifact auditors look for.

Current contract: no `counterparty_attestation` value in the `source_type` enum.

Smallest honest change: add `counterparty_attestation` to the `source_type` enum. Non-breaking, additive. `document_ledger` then tracks "attestation received YYYY-MM-DD per counterparty" with no further surface change.

### B9. Cargo mapped to the Russia Critical Items list

Pain: OFAC FFI advisory:

> "Foreign financial institutions should use this list of specified items for the purpose of mitigating sanctions risk under Treasury's sanctions authorities described in this advisory."

The Russia Critical Items Determination list (machine tools, semiconductor manufacturing equipment, electronic test equipment, propellants and precursors, lubricants, bearings, advanced optical systems, navigation instruments) is the structured cargo-risk taxonomy FFIs are told to map against. The current `cargo: string` is free-text — the contract cannot tell whether "industrial equipment" includes a specified item.

Current contract: `cargo` is a single free-text string.

Smallest honest change: borderline. Two options:

- Worker-layer first: surface `evidence_gaps` entry "Cargo not yet mapped against Russia Critical Items Determination list (Treasury, 2023-12-22)" when an HS-code or critical-items mapping source is not supplied.
- Contract-layer only if practitioners want it structurally exposed: optional `cargo_critical_items_check: { checked: boolean, list_version: string, match_status: enum }` on the request. Surfaces whether the user has mapped cargo, not whether a match was found.

Recommend worker-layer first; promote to contract only if real practitioners confirm they want this structurally exposed.

### B10. Seven-factor "significance" checklist surface

Pain: the FFI's defensibility under EO 14114 hinges on whether OFAC could later deem a transaction "significant" under the FAQ 1151 seven-factor test (see A1). Today the contract returns a single `decision_readiness_score` (0-100 heuristic) and a `risk_signal`. A practitioner preparing a file for a sanctions committee wants visibility on which of the seven factors the supplied evidence covers — and which it does not.

Current contract: no per-factor surface; `exposure_layers.foreign_sanctions_exposure_layer[]` is a flat array of strings.

Smallest honest change: optional `ffi_significance_test_indicators: string[]` on the response — a checklist mapped to the FAQ 1151 seven factors (size / number / frequency, nature, awareness / pattern, nexus to blocked persons, deceptive practices, national-security impact, other relevant factors). Same pattern and boundary as `vessel_due_diligence_indicators` and `reexport_control_indicators`: an evidence-gap checklist routed to human review, not a significance determination, and it does not change the `decision_readiness_score`. Drawn explicitly from FAQ 1151.

### D1. Front-company anatomy — corroborates B3, B6, B7

> "Russian beneficiaries routinely establish Kazakh front companies that procure electronics and drones from Europe, China, and the U.S."
>
> "patterns: newly registered LLCs, Russian beneficial owners, Kazakh nominal directors"

Source: Lansing Institute (2025-12-01).

The three-component anatomy (newly-registered + Russian UBO + Kazakh nominal director) sits at the exact intersection of:

- **B7** (counterparty `date_of_formation`) — "newly registered"
- **B6** (counterparty `specified_sectors[]`) — electronics / drones procurement
- **B3** (ownership-chain indicators) — "Russian beneficial owners, Kazakh nominal directors"

The nominal-director / UBO-jurisdiction mismatch is a new nuance inside B3's indicator checklist (e.g. "Director jurisdiction ≠ UBO jurisdiction flag"), not a separate B candidate.

### D2. Sector concentration and trade-volume jump — corroborates B6, B9

> "microelectronics, drones, and dual-use components essential for Russia's defense-industrial base"
>
> "Microchips: from $245,000 in 2021 to $18 million in 2022"

Source: Lansing Institute.

The OFAC FAQ 1148 sector list (technology, defense, construction, aerospace, manufacturing) maps directly onto "microelectronics + dual-use components." Reinforces **B6** (sector tagging) and **B9** (cargo against Russia Critical Items list). The $245k → $18M trade jump is the kind of public-side statistic the buyer-facing copy can quote later — not a pain candidate.

### D3. Goods diverted in transit — sharpens reexport_control_indicators, parks a marginal candidate

> "Multiple sources in the Central Asian re-export business told RFE/RL reporters that dual-use goods ordered by Kazakh and Kyrgyz buyers from Western suppliers are, in fact, dropped off in transit to Russian clients"

Source: CEPA.

The supplied invoice / customs / end-user statement all show Kazakhstan; the goods never arrive there. This is exactly the gap `reexport_control_indicators` already names (end-use consistency, onward-destination disclosure, order-vs-destination match) and is the practitioner-language reason that checklist exists.

Marginal candidate parked: an explicit `delivery_confirmation_source` value in the `source_type` enum (e.g. KZ customs import declaration showing goods were received and warehoused in KZ). Would close the gap structurally, but `customs_or_regulatory_source` plus `end_user_or_reexport_evidence` already cover the same evidence shape. Hold until a direct practitioner explicitly asks for it.

### D4. Ownership-chain forensics — corroborates B3

> "The listed founders and directors of the Latvian firm are Russian citizens who have served in various roles in the Russian supplier ITC Electronics"

Source: CEPA.

A concrete ownership-chain investigation anatomy: a non-Russian (Latvian) registered firm whose directors have prior employment at a Russian sanctioned-adjacent entity. **B3** ownership-chain indicators should include "Director / UBO prior employment with sanctioned-adjacent entities checked" — sharpens the gap-candidate language without changing its shape.

### D5. KZ bank wiring into Russian payment rails — operationalises A2, A6 and the OFAC "high-risk FFI" example

> "Kazakhstan's Nurbank has become the first financial institution in the country to join the Russian money transfer system Kwikpay"

Source: KIAR.

This is the OFAC FFI advisory's "high-risk FFI" example made concrete (PDF page 5: "A small bank in a jurisdiction with a strong trading relationship with Russia ... maintains relationships with multiple designated Russian banks for the purpose of facilitating trade with Russia in the local currency"). For a KZ counterparty or correspondent of such a bank, `exposure_layers.foreign_sanctions_exposure_layer` and `counterparty_readiness.outstanding_documents` are the structural answer the contract already provides.
