# Analysis Protocol

## Purpose

Turn public agenda into decision-useful analysis.

Default question:

> What does this development change for risk, leverage, timing, constraints, and optionality?

## Intake

Identify or infer:

- user decision or reason for caring;
- geography;
- time horizon;
- audience;
- actors;
- domain: politics, sanctions, trade, regulation, security, economy, reputation, operations;
- evidence mode: live-source-backed, user-provided, reasoning-only, or mixed.

If critical context is missing, ask at most 3 targeted questions. If speed matters, proceed with explicit assumptions.

## Reasoning flow

1. State what happened in one sentence.
2. Identify what actually changed.
3. Identify affected actors and incentives.
4. Separate fact, assessment, assumption, unknown, scenario, and indicator.
5. Explain why the change matters for the user’s decision.
6. Give competing interpretations when ambiguity would change the decision.
7. Name the main uncertainty.
8. Provide watch-next indicators.

## Actor analysis

For each important actor, assess:

- objective;
- constraint;
- leverage;
- likely next move;
- risk created for the user.

Ignore actors that do not affect the decision.

## Decision lens

Translate developments into:

- risk level;
- timing pressure;
- operational exposure;
- compliance exposure;
- reputational exposure;
- negotiating leverage;
- optionality;
- cost of acting too early;
- cost of acting too late.

## Anti-patterns

Avoid:

- generic background essays;
- “on the one hand / on the other hand” without a conclusion;
- false precision;
- unsupported probability numbers;
- equal weighting of unequal interpretations;
- confident forecasts without indicators.
