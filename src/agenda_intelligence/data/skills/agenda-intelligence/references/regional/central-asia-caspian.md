# Central Asia + Caspian Regional Lens

## Purpose

Use this lens when an agenda item involves Central Asia, the Caspian system, sanctions routing, trade corridors, energy, critical minerals, banking, logistics, state capacity, or Russia-China-West competition around the region.

This is not a full country-risk memo. It is a portable regional thinking layer for AI agents.

Default question:

> What does this development change for routing, compliance exposure, state leverage, corridor economics, external power competition, banking/payment risk, resource value capture, regime stability, and investor/operator optionality?

## Geographic scope

Default countries:

- Kazakhstan
- Uzbekistan
- Kyrgyzstan
- Tajikistan
- Turkmenistan
- Azerbaijan when Caspian or corridor dynamics matter

Include the wider Caspian system when it affects:

- flows;
- chokepoints;
- sanctions/compliance exposure;
- energy/logistics routing;
- value capture;
- strategic competition.

Do not force Caspian analysis when the link is weak.

## Required regional checks

Always check whether the development affects:

1. Russia-linked exposure
2. China-linked leverage
3. Western sanctions/compliance pressure
4. Middle Corridor / Trans-Caspian routing
5. Caspian energy, port, shipping, insurance, or pipeline chokepoints
6. Banking, payments, correspondent relationships, or de-risking
7. Elite, SOE, or national champion incentives
8. Border, customs, licensing, or informal rent friction
9. Resource nationalism, local content, or critical minerals policy
10. Infrastructure bottlenecks and implementation capacity
11. Turkey/Azerbaijan corridor politics where relevant
12. Gulf capital and alternative financing where relevant

## Regional signal upgrade markers

Upgrade signal strength when the item includes:

- customs, transit, tariff, or border-rule changes;
- bank de-risking, payment disruption, or correspondent banking pressure;
- sanctions designation, enforcement action, export-control action, or delisting;
- major rail, port, pipeline, road, or logistics investment;
- Caspian shipping, port, insurance, or energy disruption;
- SOE restructuring, elite-linked asset movement, or strategic asset transfer;
- Russia, China, EU, US, Turkey, Azerbaijan, or Gulf pressure on the same issue;
- critical minerals policy, export restriction, or offtake agreement;
- public procurement or infrastructure deadline with budget attached;
- repeated signals from separate regional governments.

## Regional downgrade markers

Downgrade signal strength when the item is:

- generic multi-vector rhetoric without implementation path;
- summit language without budget, legal text, or deadline;
- single-source rumor about elite politics;
- recycled corridor optimism without capacity change;
- symbolic foreign-policy positioning with no operational mechanism;
- already priced into sanctions/compliance or investor assumptions.

## Actor map

Consider only actors that materially change the answer:

- presidential administrations and ruling elites;
- national governments and regulators;
- SOEs, national champions, and elite-linked firms;
- banks, payment intermediaries, and compliance gatekeepers;
- customs, border, port, rail, and logistics operators;
- energy, mining, uranium, oil, gas, and critical minerals companies;
- Russia-linked commercial, financial, and logistics networks;
- China-linked capital, infrastructure, and trade actors;
- EU/US regulators, sanctions authorities, and export-control agencies;
- Turkey and Azerbaijan corridor actors;
- Gulf capital and alternative financing actors where relevant;
- multilateral lenders where implementation capacity matters.

For each important actor, assess:

- objective;
- constraint;
- leverage;
- likely next move;
- risk or opportunity created for the user.

## Flow map

When relevant, identify affected flows:

- money;
- goods;
- energy;
- minerals;
- data;
- people;
- influence;
- technology;
- insurance and compliance permissions.

Then state where friction, leverage, or rent capture concentrates.

## Default output

```markdown
**Bottom line:** ...
**Regional signal classification:** noise / weak signal / signal / structural shift / trigger event / compliance-relevant development
**What changed regionally:** ...
**Affected flows:** money / goods / energy / minerals / data / people / influence / technology
**Leverage shift:** ...
**Compliance exposure:** ...
**Corridor implications:** ...
**Actor incentives:** ...
**Main uncertainty:** ...
**Watch next:** ...
```

## Watch-next indicators

Prefer concrete indicators:

- draft legal text or implementing regulation;
- customs rule change;
- bank guidance or account closures;
- sanctions/export-control enforcement action;
- budget allocation;
- tender, procurement award, or construction milestone;
- port/rail/pipeline throughput change;
- insurance or shipping constraint;
- SOE transaction or asset transfer;
- public statement followed by agency action;
- repeated private-sector compliance behavior.

## Anti-patterns

Avoid:

- treating Central Asia as only a Russia-China-West chessboard;
- ignoring local elite, SOE, and bureaucratic incentives;
- assuming corridor announcements equal corridor capacity;
- over-reading summit language;
- turning every item into sanctions analysis;
- ignoring implementation capacity;
- confusing geopolitical importance with near-term user impact.
