# Iran Sanctions Adjacency Risk: Gulf Hub Crude Sourcing via Trading Houses
For: Sanctions Compliance & Trade Finance Leadership | Horizon: 6-12 months

## The Core Risk Architecture

Gulf trading hubs — particularly Fujairah, Sohar, and Duqm — are structurally dual-use. They serve as legitimate crude blending and storage centers and as the primary transshipment layer for Iranian crude evading OFAC/EU sanctions. A European refiner cannot cleanly separate these flows at the commodity level without active counterparty governance.

The risk is not that you are buying Iranian crude knowingly. It is that your trading-house intermediary is, and your institution becomes the downstream nexus of a sanctions evasion chain.

## Specific Exposure Vectors

| Vector | Mechanism | Detectability |
|---|---|---|
| Origin blending | Iranian crude commingled with Iraqi, Basra, or Omani grades in storage; re-certificated as non-sanctioned origin | Low without independent cargo inspection |
| AIS manipulation | Vessels carrying Iranian crude conduct ship-to-ship transfers in UAE/Oman waters, then appear in AIS as originating from legitimate ports | Medium — gaps in AIS history are a red flag |
| Trading house layering | Entity A (clean) buys from Entity B (Iran-linked) which bought from Entity C (IRGC-affiliated); your contract is with A | Very low without UBO and network tracing |
| Shell financing chains | Trade finance flows through correspondent banks touching OFAC-designated entities two or three hops removed | Low — surfaces only in bank compliance screening |
| Document fraud | Forged certificates of origin, falsified cargo manifests, re-flagging | Medium — detectable via consistent cargo analytics |

## Regulatory & Enforcement Environment: Next 6-12 Months

Elevated risk, not diminished.

- OFAC enforcement posture: Secondary sanctions pressure on non-US persons has intensified since 2018 and has not materially relaxed.
- EU own-account sanctions: EU Regulation 267/2012 (Iran) binds European refiners directly, independent of OFAC.
- Designation creep into UAE: OFAC has increasingly designated UAE-registered entities, front companies, and individuals.
- Geopolitical volatility: Any escalation in US-Iran nuclear negotiations historically triggers a designation surge within 60-90 days.
- UK OFSI alignment: Post-Brexit, OFSI has mirrored and in some cases accelerated Iran designations.

## Decision Framework

Maintain (acceptable if all of the following are true):
- Trading house has audited, documented UBO structure with no Iran-linked shareholders or affiliates
- Cargo-level STS transfer history and AIS continuity for all purchased parcels
- Independent pre-load inspection on origin
- Trading house has current, clean OFAC/EU screening
- Trade finance bank has confirmed counterparty passes their own EDD
- Contractual reps & warranties explicitly covering origin, no-Iran, no-STS, no-AIS-gap

If any of these are absent, you are not in "maintain" — you are in unacknowledged risk.

Deepen Due Diligence. Trigger this if:
- Trading house is <3 years old, recently renamed, or has opaque ownership
- Cargo history shows AIS gaps >12 hours in UAE/Oman/Gulf of Oman waters
- Price is consistently at an anomalous discount vs comparable benchmarks
- Trading house uses sub-agents or brokers you cannot independently verify
- Banking counterparties include any entity on UAE's own AML/sanctions lists

Specific enhanced DD steps:
1. Third-party UBO and network mapping (Kroll, K2 Integrity, Sayari) — ~$15-25K
2. Vessel history reports (MarineTraffic AIS + Windward or Pole Star analytics)
3. Insert contractual audit rights with termination right if refused
4. Escalate trade finance to bank with dedicated maritime/commodity sanctions desk

Restructure or Exit. Required if:
- Enhanced DD reveals Iran-linked ownership, affiliate, or recent designated-entity relationship
- Counterparty resists DD requests
- Trade finance bank downgrades or exits the relationship
- Volume from this counterparty represents >15-20% of crude slate

Restructuring options:
- Source direct from NOCs (ADNOC, OmanOil) — premium may be justified
- Introduce reputable major trading house (Vitol, Trafigura, Gunvor)
- Insert independent inspection agents at origin

## The Decision Most Likely Warranted

For most European refiners with existing Gulf hub/trading house relationships established 2019-2022: structured deepening of due diligence, now, before the 6-month window closes.

The cost of enhanced DD is low. The cost of a secondary sanctions designation or even an OFAC subpoena is existential for a refinery's crude procurement function. The absence of a confirmed violation in your current supply chain is not evidence of clean exposure; it is evidence that you have not yet looked hard enough.
